# Faceless Long-Form YouTube Niche Research Blueprint

Research date: **1 October 2026 UTC / 2 October 2026 in Pakistan**. Scope: English, faceless, long-form channels suitable for a new or small creator seeking fast growth, sustainable topic supply and practical AI-assisted production. This is a research and design proposal. No MCP implementation was inspected or modified.

Read with **YouTube_Evidence_Ledger.md** (primary video sources, actual channel cases and claim decisions) and **MCP_Feasibility_Matrix.md** (criterion-by-criterion collection and scoring specification). These companion files form one report.

Evidence labels used throughout: **Fact** = documented capability/policy; **Observation** = what was returned or inspected in a stated sample; **Inference** = an interpretation with alternatives; **Heuristic** = a proposed operating rule requiring validation; **Anecdote** = a creator's account without independent causal verification. Missing information remains unknown.

## 1. Executive Summary

Surface a niche when **multiple independent, reasonably comparable small or recent entrants repeatedly attract worthwhile reach; ordinary uploads have an acceptable floor; demand spans several topics; and the creator can produce a distinct, credible version consistently**. A lone viral video, a revenue estimate, a high views/subscribers ratio or few keyword results is insufficient.

The essential upgrade is a **cohort research system**, not another winner feed. It must include weak performers, record discovery provenance, compare videos at the same age where history exists, inspect the next five and ten uploads, and show coverage/confidence separately from opportunity. Production and rights feasibility are gates, not afterthoughts.

The NexLev evidence review includes 32 educational videos through selected transcript passages, 14 source families, 12 actual channel cases, 454 retrieved video IDs, six case transcript openings, two short visual samples and 40 deidentified comments. The sample shows both repeat reach and heavily skewed catalogs. It does not estimate platform-wide success probability. Details, incomplete coverage and provider discrepancies are in the ledger.

Three current API details materially change the design: revised quota buckets, a 2026 public-view definition break, and conditional permission for derived analytics/history. Treat permissions and source definitions as part of technical feasibility. Do not assume an existing MCP is approved for every proposed calculation.

## 2. What Actually Makes a Good Faceless YouTube Niche

Define a niche as **viewer need + subject boundary + repeatable format + truthful promise + feasible production method**. “History” or “business” alone is too broad; a narrow keyword alone can miss the audience's adjacent interests.

A suitable profile has: current demand across independent creators; a credible entry route without fame, exclusive access or an expensive footage library; multiple repeatable topic families; strong ideas that can sustain the intended runtime; practical visual evidence; manageable verification and asset costs; and room for a distinct contribution. AI may assist drafting, organization and visuals, but someone must verify sources, direct the story, maintain continuity and deliver originality.

Fast initial reach and a durable business are separate outcomes. A timely documentary might grow quickly but require constant news work. A technical tutorial can compound more slowly but demand continual software updates. A sleepy compilation may run for hours yet depend on someone else's voice or footage. Select the operating model deliberately.

## 3. Complete Criteria Taxonomy

| Domain | Questions to answer | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | Is attention recurring, distributed and relevant to this audience? | Matched-age reach, independent creators, topic families, questions |
| Entry opportunity | Have comparable entrants succeeded repeatedly? | Entry cohorts, size at publication when available, follow-ups |
| Competition | What must a new creator outperform or differentiate? | Active supply, entrant outcomes, concentration, quality/rights barriers |
| Repeatability / downside | What happens outside the selected hits? | Floor quantiles, misses, top-share, next five/ten |
| Content durability | Can ideas keep working and keep being generated? | Topic inventory, older-video increments, seasonality, update burden |
| Packaging / viewing structure | Is there a repeatable truthful promise and natural next video? | Titles, inspected thumbnails, transcript/series structure |
| Production / fit | Can this creator repeatedly deliver the required evidence and visuals? | Resource brief, timed pilot, asset plan, skill/expertise rubric |
| Risk | Can the intended method respect rights, policy and factual accuracy? | Asset-specific rights, current policies, sourcing review |
| Evidence quality | How much is known, comparable and representative of the retrieved cohort? | Coverage, provenance, precision, history, sensitivity |

The feasibility matrix assigns every criterion to five tiers: **1 Essential**, **2 High Value**, **3 Advanced**, **4 Optional**, **5 Do Not Use**. “Essential” does not mean universally accessible: an essential input can instead require an explicit unknown or permission state.

## YouTube Research Findings — Direct Channel Study Added After Section 3

The research treats educational/meta advice and actual-channel observations as separate evidence streams. Its source priority follows the updated prompt exactly. The detailed ledger includes claims, timestamps, dates, methods, metrics, tools, thresholds, useful signals, red flags and classifications, followed by channel cases and a **YouTube-to-MCP Conversion Table**.

The main observational correction is that **small-channel success is possible without establishing niche-wide accessibility**. The Forgotten Britain and SPACE_LABb samples have prominent hits alongside low medians. Wealth Chronicles has several later successes but immature follow-up outcomes. The Invisible Crown has four strong uploads, with too few subsequent releases to validate ongoing repeatability. History Profiles is an established reference; Free Documentary–Space describes a professional studio operation. None should be treated as an interchangeable beginner benchmark.

Advice converges around audience coherence, outlier research, adaptation and production fit. Advice is mixed on narrowness, search versus browse, launch quantity and series tactics. Revenue headlines, fixed subscriber/age filters, “the algorithm favors this” explanations and mandatory runtimes are not validated universal rules. This is an observational consistency check; it does not establish the causal effectiveness of gurus' methods.

## 4. Demand Signals

Use three layers. **Reach:** median and upper quantiles at a fixed publication age, alongside an absolute floor linked to the creator's goal. **Breadth:** independent channels and topic families achieving that reach. **Persistence:** demand across distinct windows or incremental views of older uploads, where valid history exists. Display these separately before combining them.

A niche with a few famous-channel hits has visible attention but weak entrant evidence. A niche with many small channels repeatedly achieving useful reach is more relevant to this user's objective. A tiny channel's 1,000-view upload can be a large relative outlier yet still be below the desired absolute outcome.

YouTube does not provide public competitor keyword-search volume. Google Trends can add directional context; its index is sampled and normalized, not a count of YouTube searches or viewers. Use the YouTube-search property where applicable and preserve query, geography, period and normalization context. [S15]

## 5. Competition Signals

Separate **observed supply**, **effective competing supply**, **concentration** and **entry barriers**. Ten sophisticated competitors with exceptional archives can be harder to challenge than a hundred repetitive uploads. High demand and high supply can still contain a useful differentiated opening. Low supply can mean low demand or incomplete discovery.

Use a stable topic boundary and repeated discovery protocol before comparing supply over time. Search totals and a provider's indexed channel count are not platform censuses. Track newly observed qualifying channels, active creators, upload rates, repeated packaging mechanisms and the outcomes of entrants. Distinguish a newly discovered old channel from a newly active entrant.

Competition conclusions should read “the observed cohort has X active creators and Y recent entrant outcomes,” with coverage and alternatives. Avoid an unsupported universal “saturation percentage.”

## 6. Small/New Channel Opportunity

Report current subscriber bands: under 1k; 1–10k; 10–50k; 50–100k; 100k–1M; above 1M; hidden/unknown. Use the bands to stratify, not as a universal cutoff. For a fast-growth beginner screen, emphasize under 50k and under 100k as sensitivity alternatives.

Separate account creation, first public upload, first observed in-niche upload, and first snapshot. A reused or old account can be a new entrant to a subject. Public subscriber counts are rounded down to three significant figures and may be hidden; creation date is not niche-entry date. [S5]

“Small at breakout” requires a historical subscriber observation near publication. Current size is a post-outcome measurement. Without historical size, label **currently small / size at publication unknown**. Do not reconstruct the earlier subscriber count from today's views.

Use entry-age bands 0–6, 6–12, 12–24 and 24–36 months, with a separate uncertain-entry flag. Compare entrants at matched opportunity/exposure, and preserve stopped or weak channels rather than dropping them from the denominator.

## 7. Breakout/Outlier Methodology

Keep three distinct measurements: **within-channel lift**, **comparable-peer standing** and **absolute goal attainment**. A provider's video outlier ratio and channel outlier score may use different baselines; record their names and definitions. Never merge them into one unexplained number.

Use a leave-one-out baseline: exclude the candidate from its own reference set. Match age, format, subject and relevant era. Prefer log views and robust summaries, with partial pooling when the channel has few uploads. A cold-start lifetime-view comparison can generate leads but cannot recreate first-week performance.

Record the cohort and scoring version before examining winners. A within-niche top-decile cutoff mechanically produces a top decile; it cannot by itself show that one niche has more demand than another. Use an external, declared reference cohort or an independent absolute goal for cross-niche comparisons.

## 8. Failure Floor and Survivorship Bias

Show median, 25th percentile, low-view fraction, top-one/top-three view shares and number of independent creators. Keep channel-balanced and video-weighted results side by side: a prolific channel must not silently dominate the niche.

Distinguish **upload miss**, **weak repeatability**, **stagnation in a recorded period**, **no recent public uploads** and **permanent failure**. Only the first four can often be observed. Creator intention, deleted/private videos, external traffic, pivot reasons and total profitability are usually unknown.

For every candidate breakout, track the next five and ten eligible uploads in chronological order. Evaluate their reach at the same age, and separately annotate continued topic/format alignment. If fewer uploads exist, show partial follow-up. If uploads are too young, show **pending maturity**. A stop can be recorded as no observed follow-up by a time horizon, not automatically zero-view videos.

Sample weaknesses are themselves findings. The present case cohort was deliberately contrasted and provider-selected; its miss rate is not a niche failure probability. A proper prospective entrant panel should be assembled before future outcomes are known.

## 9. Faceless Viability

Faceless means a recurring on-camera host is not central to the intended production. It does not mean anonymous, personality-free, unskilled or legally reusable. Voice, analytical judgment, credibility, visual identity and original access can be the moat.

Classify intended styles separately: original screen recording; original maps/charts; illustrated narrative; licensed documentary/stock; original gameplay/simulation; hands-only demonstration; public-domain assets with original analysis; or compilations requiring third-party permissions. Mark mixed styles and uncertain labels.

Provider classification is a lead. Check representative openings, middle sections and later uploads before assigning a channel-wide label. This review visually inspected only two short samples; all other faceless classifications remain provider-assisted or tentative. Named experts or celebrities in a faceless compilation do not remove personality/rights dependency.

## 10. Long-Form Viability

For this blueprint, start with an **8-minute minimum** as a user-goal research heuristic, then sensitivity-test 6 and 10 minutes. Split 8–15, 15–30, 30–60 and 60+ minutes rather than treating all long videos as equivalent. Exclude live streams and clearly identified Shorts from the main cohort; keep uncertain format separate.

API search duration `long` means over 20 minutes. Search `medium` plus `long`, or search without a duration filter and post-filter, to cover the intended range. Language/region parameters are not strict audience filters. [S6]

Runtime cannot establish retention or watch time. Check whether the topic supports evidence, escalation, explanation or practical steps for the chosen length. Official education does not establish a universal best runtime or mandatory upload cadence. [S13] Vertical or square videos up to three minutes can qualify as Shorts under current rules; duration alone is an incomplete Shorts classifier. [S14]

## 11. Topic Depth and Repeatability

Create at least 30 distinct, credible topic premises spanning at least three families before giving a strong practical endorsement. This is a planning heuristic, not a statistically validated requirement. Each premise needs a viewer payoff, source path and feasible visual plan. Rewording one event into many titles does not count as depth.

Map entity × tension/question × period/context × explanatory mechanism. For business documentaries this might cover incentives, financing, operations, competition and consequences. For tutorials, distinguish real user tasks and software versions. Reject inventories that depend on invented facts, endless rumor or a single news event.

Count validated families and feasible premises; do not use LLM output length as the score. Human-check a subset and record duplicate/unsupported rates. Track exhausted families and rising research costs after the first ten releases.

## 12. Evergreen / Trend / Shelf-Life

Separate topic durability from observed video performance. A timeless subject can have a poorly performing video; a trending subject can create an enduring explanation. Label timely event, cyclical/seasonal, update-sensitive utility, durable explanation, or mixed.

True shelf-life needs increments on the same videos across time. A currently popular three-year-old video might have earned almost all views at launch. Current lifetime counts cannot prove ongoing demand. Compare older-video increments with new-release increments and record refresh/revival events.

Use at least one seasonal cycle before a confident seasonality claim; two are preferable. Distinguish demand shifts, creator upload changes, discovery changes and counting-regime breaks. Trend urgency should include the time required to research, source visuals and publish credibly.

## 13. Content-Gap Methodology

A useful gap combines **positive demand evidence**, **an inadequately served viewer need** and **a feasible differentiated treatment**. Candidate gaps include missing explanations, dated tutorials, inaccessible jargon, unaddressed objections, absent regional context or poor evidence/visual clarity.

For each gap, record the need, existing alternatives, proof of interest, proposed difference, production path and plausible reason incumbents have not filled it. Comments may reveal objections, but a single request is not sufficient validation. A gap caused by scarce evidence or expensive rights can be unattractive.

Use related audiences and mechanisms to expand discovery without pretending to know actual audience overlap. Confirm the gap across more than one query and more than one creator. Few exact keyword matches should trigger adjacent research, not automatic approval.

## 14. Titles, Thumbnails and Packaging

Separate topic demand, presentation mechanism and visual execution. Title mechanisms can include hidden cause, costly mistake, transformation, comparison, investigation, constraint or an unresolved question. A title cluster is an annotation, not proof of why the video succeeded.

Inspect thumbnails at realistic display sizes. Review hierarchy, clarity, relevance, contrast, complementarity with the title, truthful stakes and reliance on recognizable people. Similarity can reveal crowded conventions, but distinct colors alone do not establish an opening.

Competitor CTR and impressions are unavailable publicly. Before/after title or thumbnail changes are observational and confounded by time and distribution. Owner-controlled A/B tests can support packaging decisions; YouTube's testing evaluates watch-time outcomes, not simply highest CTR. [S16] Label any generated title/thumb recommendations as hypotheses, and preserve rights and factual accuracy.

## 15. Series/Binge Potential

Look for recurring viewer payoff and a natural next question, while keeping each entry understandable to a new viewer. Separate an episodic narrative, recurring investigation format, learning sequence and topic anthology. Numbering alone is weak evidence.

Public playlists and repeated title patterns reveal structure; they do not reveal actual viewing sessions, end-screen CTR or returning viewers. A series score should measure coherence and feasible follow-up ideas, then show observed outcomes separately. Test whether later episodes retain comparable-age reach without assuming audience overlap.

The reviewed “dynamic series,” content-wall and five-video launch advice belongs in an experimental planning module. It must not become a platform rule or a guaranteed growth multiplier.

## 16. Production / Visual / Research Burden

Score research difficulty, source accessibility, fact-checking, scripting, visual evidence, asset licensing, narration, editing/animation, update pressure and required expertise separately. Assess against the creator's actual resources; absent resource details, show a conditional assessment, not a universal “easy.”

Use a short pilot to record hours and costs, then estimate the intended video. Track revision/verification effort as well as generation speed. The two visual samples demonstrate different burdens: maps and sourced b-roll versus illustrated historical scenes. Neither provides a verified production budget.

Plan AI assistance task by task: outlines from verified sources; alternate explanations; scene boards; original illustrations; caption drafts; continuity checks. Human review is required for factual claims, source attribution, misleading imagery and the final promise. A cheap first upload that cannot be repeated at the target cadence is not viable.

## 17. Copyright / Policy / Monetization Risks

YouTube renamed repetitious content as **inauthentic content** in July 2025, clarifying mass-produced/repetitive treatment; reused-content rules remain distinct. AI use itself is not an automatic disqualifier, and faceless content is not automatically eligible. Evaluate original contribution and the channel-wide production pattern. [S17]

Realistic altered/synthetic material can require disclosure; the official explanation says disclosure itself does not limit monetization or audience reach. This does not guarantee approval or excuse misleading claims. [S18] Advertiser suitability is contextual, especially for violence, sensitive events and controversial subject matter. It is a risk assessment, not a public measurement of competitor ad status. [S19]

Use asset-specific permissions. Public availability, credits, a short clip or an old-looking photograph does not establish reuse rights. Fair use is context-specific rather than a seconds rule. [S20] Wikimedia and Smithsonian assets can help, but verify each asset's license/conditions and attribution. [S21–S22]

**API policy fact:** base rules restrict derived metrics and cross-owner aggregation. Section III.L introduces a route for audited analytics developers that explicitly apply for the additional allowances. [S1] Accepted additional-metrics clients can create qualifying scores/ratios/comparisons and retain specified statistical/derived data for up to 36 calendar months; textual metadata still follows the 30-day refresh/deletion requirement. [S2] The user's MCP approval status was not established. Implementation should therefore verify accepted scope before enabling these features. Provider access is a separate licensing/provenance question, not an automatic exemption.

## 18. Historical Signals

Historical observations enable velocity, acceleration, cohort maturation, earlier channel size, repeatability, catalog contribution and supply change. Store observation time, metric definition, source, precision and collection gaps. Never manufacture earlier values from today's totals.

Current docs say all-format public views began counting at playback start—including hover/autoplay—from **24 August 2026**; the revision announcement is dated 27 August. Mark this regime transition. Older cumulative counts plus new increments are not a clean same-definition series, and no universal conversion factor is established. [S3–S4]

Academic work provides reasons to avoid simplistic curves: search samples vary by query/order/time [S23]; archived platform datasets have coverage limitations [S24]; historical video trajectories have multiple phases [S26]. These support careful sampling, not a current universal decay curve. The inspected NexLev daily history is supplemental and unvalidated for exact causal attribution.

## 19. Geo/Language Opportunities

For English content, verify actual audio language where available; titles and metadata can disagree. NexLev's `englishOnly` video-search flag is an ASCII-title filter, not a reliable spoken-English detector. Keep language confidence and multilingual content separate.

Do not infer viewer geography from creator country, viewability in a region or English titles. A region/language opportunity is a hypothesis grounded in localized questions, sparse relevant treatments and independently observed demand. Compare matched query/task samples with adequate coverage and note translation differences.

No public rival RPM, viewer-country mix or income profile is available from these basic counts. Language adaptations require local factual context, pronunciation, rights and a verification plan, not only machine translation.

## 20. New Criteria We Had Not Considered

Add **at-publication size**, **entry versus account age**, **post-hit topic drift**, **follow-up censoring**, **absolute goal alongside relative outlier**, **query sensitivity**, **provider disagreement**, **date precision**, **counting-regime version**, **catalog/source coverage**, **visual evidence density**, **source-access moat**, **named-person dependency**, **update debt**, **idea inventory validity**, **production revision burden**, **rights feasibility**, **cohort concentration** and **cross-platform transfer limits**.

These prevent common category errors: a newly indexed old account is not a new creator; a four-video sample is not a 69-video channel; a 100× ratio need not be useful reach; an attractive format can require unreachable assets; and a new channel can be promising before it is mature enough to judge.

## 21. Red Flags / Automatic Rejection Conditions

Hard rejection applies to the **proposed creator plan** when it depends on unavailable rights, fabricated evidence, deceptive impersonation, a production requirement clearly beyond resources, or private competitor data presented as public fact. Record the reason and evidence. A policy-dependent feature without permission is disabled, not silently implemented.

Use **research more** for incomplete discovery, few uploads, hidden size, immature follow-ups, uncertain faceless/language classification or conflicting providers. Use **weak opportunity under this goal** for a mature observed cohort with low absolute reach or poor entrant repeatability. Neither state justifies declaring the entire niche impossible.

High concentration, relentless update pressure, celebrity dependence, one-event dependence, repeated sensational claims, expensive assets and mass-produced sameness are review triggers. They can be manageable with a distinct, feasible treatment; do not turn every qualitative flag into an automatic platform-policy accusation.

## 22. Reliability / Confidence Framework

Report confidence by dimension: coverage; number of independent channels; comparability; history maturity; metadata precision; classification quality; query sensitivity; source agreement. Each dimension should carry a measured input and a plain-language reason. Confidence is separate from opportunity.

Use channel-cluster resampling for descriptive uncertainty where sufficient channels exist. A Wilson interval can describe a binary channel hit fraction under its statistical assumptions, but cannot repair selection bias. [S27] A conventional modified-z label relies on distributional assumptions and a nonzero scale; do not mechanically treat a skewed view-count sample as normally distributed. [S28]

Proposed confidence grades: **High** only with broad independent coverage, comparable mature outcomes, stable conclusions and verified critical fields; **Moderate** for useful breadth with known gaps; **Low** for sparse/selected/conflicting data. These are rubric grades, not 95% population confidence guarantees. A missing essential input produces an unknown state, not a zero score.

## 23. Recommended Fast-Growth Niche Framework

Run five decisions in order:

1. **Scope and feasibility:** identify the viewer need, long-form style, differentiation, rights and production plan.
2. **Demand and entry:** find independent comparable successes, including currently small creators and genuinely recent niche entrants.
3. **Downside and repetition:** inspect full sampled catalogs, ordinary outcomes, follow-up maturity and topic consistency.
4. **Durability and ideas:** test multiple topic families, timing risk, shelf-life evidence and a credible premise inventory.
5. **Evidence quality:** verify metadata and discovery coverage, run sensitivity checks, and issue an evidence-graded decision.

Output one of **Promising for a pilot**, **Promising but unproven**, **Research more**, **Weak under this goal**, or **Reject proposed method**. Include the strongest supporting evidence, the strongest counterexample, unknowns and the condition that would change the decision.

Prefer a profile with several independent small/recent repeat winners, useful ordinary-upload reach, evidence-backed ideas and original visuals the creator can sustain. The studied channels are references for testing this profile; they are not a ranked recommendation to copy their subject or style.

## 27. Features To Modify/Merge/Remove

## 28. Recommended Formulas

All formulas below are **proposed analytical designs**, not validated YouTube ranking factors. Permissions from Section 17 apply. Inputs must carry dates, definitions and missing states. Use same-age views `V_i(a)` when available; otherwise label an approximate age-bucket analysis. Examples are synthetic arithmetic, not platform measurements.

| ID | Purpose, inputs and normalization | Window / sample / interpretation | Failure mode and worked example |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Relative lift `L=(V+1)/(B+1)`, with leave-one-out age-matched robust baseline `B` | Prefer 30-day views; ≥10 references preferred, 5 exploratory; log-scale summaries | Tiny baseline inflates ratio; different ages invalidate it. `V=30,000`, `B=10,000` gives about 3×; 300 vs 100 also gives about 3× but far less reach |
| F2 | Cold-start pooling: `b=λb_channel+(1−λ)b_peer`, `λ=n/(n+k)`, baseline logs `b`, `k=10` initially | Same age/format/era; peer pool ≥30 videos across ≥10 channels preferred | Biased peer definition and rapid growth distort pooling. With `n=5`, `λ=1/3`; channel log-baseline 9 and peer 8 produce 8.33 |
| F3 | Goal attainment `H_i=1[V_i(30)≥G]`; show peer percentile separately | User-linked `G`; illustrative 10k, sensitivity 5k/25k; no automatic top-decile gate | `G` is not universal viability. 12k passes 10k and fails 25k; peer rank can be high in a weak cohort |
| F4 | Channel success fraction `Σ H_c/C`, where `H_c` means a declared repeated-goal outcome | Mature entry cohort; ≥20 independent channels exploratory, ≥30 preferred; Wilson descriptive interval | Selection and channel dependence remain. 6/20=30%; approximate Wilson 95% interval 15–52%, not platform success probability |
| F5 | Channel-balanced floor: median across channels of their `Q25(V_i(30))`; also report video-weighted floor | ≥5 mature uploads per channel preferred; disclose fewer; compare identical entry/exposure horizons | Floors of prolific channels otherwise dominate. Channel floors 200/800/2,000 give a balanced median 800 |
| F6 | Top-hit dependence `T1=max(V)/ΣV`; also top-three share | Same-age catalog preferred; ≥10 uploads exploratory; lifetime fallback clearly labeled | Incomplete catalog and age skew inflate/deflate share. One 90k hit plus nine 1k videos gives 90.9% |
| F7 | Post-hit repeatability `R_k=ΣH_j/k` for next `k=5,10` eligible uploads | Chronological follow-ups at identical age; show matured `m/k`, pending and absent separately | A young follow-up is not a failure. Three mature hits among five gives 60%; three observed uploads cannot become 3/10 |
| F8 | Independent demand breadth: count independent qualifying creators and validated topic families; optional effective count `N_eff=1/Σs_c²` | Rolling 90-day publication cohorts, mature 30-day outcomes; ≥10 channels exploratory | Related networks are not independent. Equal shares across four creators yield effective count 4; one dominant creator lowers it |
| F9 | Cohort concentration `HHI=Σs_c²`, shares of matched-age observed reach | Fixed topic/era/window; ≥10 channels; compare sensitivity to top creator removal | Not market share or a causal barrier. Shares .6/.2/.2 give HHI .44 |
| F10 | Velocity `ΔV/Δt` using measured snapshots; acceleration `r_later−r_earlier` | Two observations for velocity, three for acceleration; 24h/7d intervals declared | Sparse discovery, corrections and counting changes. 1,000 added in 24h =41.7/hour; next interval 2,000 gives acceleration +41.7/hour |
| F11 | Demand momentum: ratio of channel-balanced matched-age median reach in two publication cohorts | Two 30/90-day cohorts after maturity; ≥10 channels per cohort preferred | Changed mix/season/regime can explain ratio. 15k versus 10k =1.5×; matched repeat-channel alternative needed |
| F12 | Observed supply change `(C_t−C_prev)/C_prev`, with stable query/eligibility protocol; upload-rate alternative | ≥2 repeated observation windows; three preferable; fixed cohort and new-entry view | Better indexing is not growth. 24 vs20 =20%, only for observed qualifying supply |
| F13 | Older-catalog contribution `ΣΔV_old/ΣΔV_all`, with fixed old-age boundary | ≥90-day-old videos; ≥30 days tracking; 10 old videos preferred | New metric regime and missing videos confound; lifetime totals do not substitute. 30k old of 100k increments =30% |
| F14 | Engagement descriptors: `1,000×likes/views`, `1,000×comments/views`; robust channel summaries | Same observation time/definition; ≥20 videos preferred; count missing engagement separately | Bots, moderation, controversy, changed view denominator. 200 likes/10k views =20 per1k; not satisfaction |
| F15 | Idea feasibility fraction = validated distinct premises / generated candidates; count usable families separately | 30 candidate premises, human-check ≥10 and every finalist | LLM redundancy/hallucination. 18 validated of 30 =60%; four families help breadth but don't prove demand |
| F16 | Production burden = estimated hours/cost by task; capacity = available production hours / hours per video | Timed pilot plus 3 topic estimates; creator-specific cadence | First-video setup and revisions vary. 24 hours/week and12 hours/video suggests2/week before admin/overhead |
| F17 | Proposed opportunity index: demand20 + entrant access25 + repetition15 + floor15 + differentiation10 + production10 + topic depth5 (weights total100) | Components normalized against declared external benchmark/rubric; run weight sensitivity | Correlated signals double-count; unknown essentials prevent scoring. Component scores70/60/50/40/60/80/80 yield60.5/100; illustrative only |
| F18 | Robustness: share of predeclared reasonable specifications giving same decision, plus decision-flip reasons | Change queries, size bands, age windows, goal floors, top-creator exclusion and weights | Agreement among biased variants is not correctness. 8/10 consistent =80% specification agreement, not80% chance of success |

F1–F3 support breakout identification; F4–F9 protect against survivorship and concentration; F10–F13 require history; F14 is contextual; F15–F16 assess feasibility; F17 is optional prioritization; F18 checks fragility. Statistical references inform uncertainty techniques [S27–S28]; weights, goals, sampling targets and cutoffs are original proposals requiring calibration.

If using log-MAD scoring, compute it on comparable `log1p(V)` values and return undefined when MAD is zero. A fixed 3.5 label is an outlier-screening convention, not proof of virality. Empirical reference percentiles or transparent lift are easier to explain for heavily skewed cohorts.

## 29. Recommended Defaults and Thresholds

| Setting | Starting heuristic | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility | English audio where verified, ≥8min VOD | Test6/10min; separate live/Shorts/unknown |
| Recent discovery | Last 90 days; contextual last 365 days | Discovery window differs from matured scoring window |
| Outcome age | 30 days; secondary7/90 days | Never backfill unavailable snapshots |
| Size emphasis | Current <50k; sensitivity<100k | At-publication size unknown unless recorded |
| Entry emphasis | First observed in-niche ≤12 months; report0–6/6–12 separately | Old account/rebrand/coverage flags |
| Absolute goal | User-set; example10k by30days | Test5k/25k; no universal profit inference |
| Relative outlier | Exploratory3× or ≥90th external-reference percentile | Require absolute result and matched baseline; no universal truth |
| Standard breadth | 30–50 channels; 300–800 eligible videos | Targets, not statistical guarantees; include weak cases |
| Deep breadth | 60–100 channels; 1,000–3,000 videos | Topic boundary, independence and coverage matter more than count |
| Strong support | ≥3 independent repeat winners, ≥3 families, meaningful ordinary floor | Proposal; insufficient alone for High confidence |
| Follow-ups | Next5 and10 | Show maturity, absence and drift; do not force denominator |
| Pilot inventory | 30 distinct premises /≥3 families | Human verification and resource feasibility |
| Floor rejection | No universal numeric cutoff | Compare creator goal and mature entrant cohort; investigate data first |
| Composite | Optional, weights shown | Missing essentials → unscored; keep confidence separate |

Calibration should use future entrants and time-separated holdouts, not optimize thresholds on the winners used to design them. Evaluate ranking usefulness, false promising decisions, discovery coverage, labor cost and stability. Report changes to defaults by version and validation period.

## 30. Recommended Data-Collection Pipeline

1. Write a resource brief and niche boundary; define reference cohorts, inclusion/exclusion rules and the absolute goal.
2. Discover through several query variants and relevance/date orders; add adjacent routes and provider indexes. Keep query, parameters, rank, source and capture time.
3. Resolve channel IDs; hydrate public channel fields; retrieve uploads playlists with pagination. Use video publish dates, not playlist-add dates. Record inaccessible entries and collection stops.
4. Hydrate eligible video IDs; parse duration; label live/Shorts/language uncertainty; deduplicate originals/reuploads conservatively; retain raw-source identifiers.
5. Construct the cohort before ranking winners. Stratify size, niche entry, upload age and format. Preserve weak performers and stopped channels.
6. Enrich only shortlisted cases with comments, transcripts and selected visual/thumbnail inspection. Deidentify commentary, limit sensitive profiling and label auxiliary-provider output.
7. Collect prospective snapshots under verified retention permissions. Enrich exact timestamps before chronological follow-up analysis.
8. Calculate permitted descriptors and proposed scores; run channel-balanced alternatives and sensitivity checks. Generate an evidence card with counterexamples and unknowns.

**Current quota facts:** search has its own default 100 calls/day at 1 per call; other relevant list methods generally cost 1 against the combined10,000-unit daily bucket. Pagination consumes another request; quotas reset at midnight Pacific. Project allocations must be checked in Cloud console. Do not use the obsolete100-general-units-per-search estimate. [S7]

`videos:batchGetStats` uses a separate granular-statistics bucket of 10,000 units/day at 1 per call. It returns supported statistics plus optional snippet/content details; its timestamp field is `publishTime`. Check failed IDs in its summary. Use a conservative configurable batch size; this research did not verify an arbitrary maximum. [S4, S8]

Search result totals are approximate, capped and unsuitable as saturation denominators. `relatedToVideoId` is deprecated; the public API does not supply the personalized recommendation graph assumed by some research workflows. Upload enumeration should use playlists rather than capped channel search. Official category/localization dictionaries can annotate metadata, but do not define a micro-niche or measure viewer geography. [S6]

An illustrative Standard plan: 12 query variants ×2 orders ×2 pages =48 search calls; 600 deduplicated video IDs at 50/request =12 video-detail requests;40 channels at 50/request =1 channel request;3 upload pages/channel =120 playlist requests;20 comment pages =20 requests. Estimated non-search total153 units, excluding retries and enrichment. This is a collection budget scenario, not proof of complete coverage. Snapshot rounds for 600 known IDs at 50/batch cost 12 in the applicable stats bucket, if supported by the deployed endpoint. Provider pricing/limits are separate and were not audited.

## 31. Recommended Historical Snapshot Strategy

Start monitoring at first discovery. Record discovery delay and distinguish **captured first day** from **first discovered weeks later**. For selected new uploads, aim at 1h,6h,24h,72h,7d,30d and90d where practical; exact first-hour conclusions require exact publish timestamps and close observation timing. Scale cadence to the creator's decision need.

Channel stats can be daily for watchlists and weekly for the broader research panel; known-video snapshots should avoid repeated keyword search. Older catalogs can use weekly intervals. Capture changes, gaps and corrections rather than assuming monotone counters. Preserve metric-definition versions across the August2026 transition.

Apply source-specific retention and refresh rules from Section17. Refreshing a current count does not turn an old historical observation into new data. Without authorized history, return **insufficient_history**; without analytics permission, return **policy_permission_required** for dependent features. Keep live metadata refresh, permitted statistical history and auxiliary licensed datasets logically distinct.

## 32. Recommended Quick / Standard / Deep Research Behavior

| Mode | Work and target coverage | Appropriate output |
|---|---|---|
| Quick | 6–8 variants;15–25 channels;100–250 eligible videos; a few contrasting cases; no claimed historical velocity without data | Discovery leads, obvious feasibility problems, Low/Moderate evidence; research-more decisions allowed |
| Standard | 30–50 channels;300–800 videos; three sorts plus upload pagination; winners/weak cases; comments and sampled production review; same-age records when available | Pilot shortlist, floor/entry/repetition panel and explicit unknowns |
| Deep | 60–100 channels;1k–3k videos; broad query/entry cohorts; source-access/asset assessment; monitored next5/10; time holdout validation | Stronger cohort comparison, robustness and uncertainty; still no guaranteed outcomes |

These are proposed research budgets. Counts do not substitute for independent sources or completeness. Stop expanding when new routes cease changing relevant conclusions and the decisive uncertainties are resolved; keep unresolved maturity/history pending. The present work is a **deep design/evidence review with selected channel cases**, not a completed representative longitudinal niche survey.

## 34. Final Checklist: Should This Niche Be Surfaced?

- Is the viewer need, topic boundary, format and intended production method clear?
- Does current demand appear across independent creators and multiple topic families?
- Is there comparable small/recent entrant evidence, with account age and at-publication size separated?
- Are relative outliers accompanied by useful absolute reach?
- Are ordinary-upload outcomes and weak/stopped cases included?
- Do the next five/ten uploads support repeatability, or are they explicitly immature/incomplete?
- Can the creator make at least30 distinct credible premises with feasible visuals and sources?
- Is differentiation truthful and practical, with manageable rights, policy and update burdens?
- Are dates, formats, language, duplicate status, metric regimes and discovery coverage checked?
- Are private competitor metrics excluded and provider estimates identified?
- Are permitted analytics/history capabilities established for the planned implementation?
- Does the recommendation survive reasonable alternative queries, thresholds and removal of the biggest winner?
- Are confidence, counterevidence, unknowns and the pilot's success/failure criteria visible?

Surface **Promising for a pilot** when these have credible support. Surface **Promising but unproven** when initial evidence is good but repetition/maturity remains open. Use **Research more** for weak evidence, **Weak under this goal** for mature unfavorable observations, and **Reject proposed method** for an infeasible or unacceptable production plan. Never substitute a numeric score for the supporting evidence.
