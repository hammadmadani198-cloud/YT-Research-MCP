# YouTube Research Evidence Ledger

Research capture: 1 October 2026 UTC (2 October in Pakistan for the later session). Public counts change. This is a source-grounded observational review, not a controlled growth experiment.

## What was actually reviewed

- 32 educational videos: metadata verified and claim-focused transcript passages reviewed through NexLev. These are **partial transcript reviews**, not 32 videos watched end to end.
- 15 publishing channels representing 14 source families: vidIQ and vidIQ Tutorials are one commercial organization. Guest interviews do not create independent experimental evidence.
- 12 actual channel cases; 454 distinct video IDs across first-page newest/popular/oldest lists, deduplicated within each channel. This is a selected, incomplete sample, not 454 fully watched videos or a channel census.
- Six actual-video transcript openings reviewed; two 0–120-second samples visually analyzed by NexLev's multimodal tool. Visual findings are provider-assisted observations and can contain errors.
- 40 newest comments across two case videos, deidentified for qualitative review. No claim that these represent all viewers.
- One provider-supplied 90-day channel history inspected. Its provenance and completeness were not independently audited.

Priority: official documentation → official creator education → actual channel/video observations → quality creator/analyst research → creator-economy/academic research → community evidence. Lower-priority sources help form hypotheses; they do not override access limits or official policy.

### Breakout follow-up: Wealth Chronicles

The oldest-list order was checked against exact video-detail timestamps for its first 12 uploads. Its first retrieved upload, published 7 September, had 21,787 views when detailed. The following five had 318, 631, 402, 182 and 654 views (median 402). The following ten add 78, 39, 845, 471 and 14,167 views (median 436.5); one of those ten exceeds 10,000 current views. These are descriptive counts at capture, **not a fixed-age repeat-hit rate**. The later successful upload is only ten days old and the earlier ones have longer exposure. No 30-day validation is yet possible. Other larger later uploads make a permanent-failure label inappropriate.

### Cases that challenge winner-only interpretation

The Forgotten Britain: [energy-crisis video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d1SD0QPD-P8), around 43k, versus a 216-view median across its retrieved 20-video sample. Britain Field: [wedding-related hit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sPRC5vNTjU), around 66k, versus 52 across the 19 retrieved IDs. SPACE_LABb: [Artemis-related hit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bdygcDw_NM8), around 222k, versus 261 across 59 retrieved IDs. These show that small-channel viral examples can coexist with weak floors. They do not establish what caused the outcomes, or the failure probability of the niche.

### Comments: evidence of questions and trust friction

Twenty newest comments on each of the bakery and Nixon videos were reviewed. The bakery sample includes anecdotes, disagreement with geographic/statistical framing and a specific historical correction. The Nixon sample includes questions about gold supply and congressional action, political disagreement and objections alleging AI content. These are leads for verification and framing, not proof of true errors, AI authorship, broad audience sentiment or retention. Political comments were not used to profile viewers. No usernames or sensitive audience attributes are retained in this deliverable.

### Provider-data discrepancies

NexLev semantic records, channel about metadata and upload-list meta do not always agree. Examples: Hidden British Heritage's old account join date versus recent observed content; Faint Signals' four returned IDs versus a much larger about count; Sleeping Economist's list meta count of 31 versus about count 120. Some list dates are derived from relative labels and cluster on the first of a month; Wealth Chronicles' first-video detail instead gives 7 September. One 90-day Britain Field history contains cumulative views/subscribers and zero-valued video fields. Zero cannot be assumed to mean no uploads. No statistical score in this research relies on that history.

The correct product response is a conflict/precision/coverage flag, source timestamp, exact-detail enrichment and an incomplete-history state. Do not silently replace missing records with zeros, call samples complete, or interpret a provider's revenue, RPM, faceless or monetization labels as private YouTube facts.

## YouTube Research Findings

**Recurring support:** audience coherence; separate topic from format; inspect recent independent small-channel successes; adapt successful mechanisms; check the viewing floor and follow-through; match production to resources. These are reasonable research directions, with varying evidence strength.

**Mixed:** broad versus narrow positioning; search versus browse discovery; multiple-video launches; series; borrowing packaging conventions. Different audience and channel stages can explain differences. The sampled cases do not identify a universal winner.

**Unsupported as universal rules:** fixed subscriber/age/monthly-view cutoffs, guaranteed revenue, a mandatory launch count, a mandatory video length, and inferring recommendation traffic or repeat viewers from public counts.

**Historical/context-bound:** 2017 algorithm education, 2020 search tactics, cross-platform follower bands and older income examples. Preserve the useful principle while rechecking current capabilities.

This review tests whether the advice is consistent with contrasting public outcomes. It does **not** validate all of the speakers' named examples, measure causal effectiveness, or produce a representative success/failure rate. A prospectively collected entry cohort is the next empirical validation step.

## Terminology mapping

| Creator term | Research meaning |
|---|---|
| Niche / avatar | Viewer need, topic boundary, format, promise and production approach; persona is a planning hypothesis |
| TAM | Observed reach ceiling clue, not counted addressable people |
| Outlier | Unusually strong observation relative to a declared baseline |
| Low competition / whitespace | A hypothesized service gap with positive demand evidence |
| Floor | Low quantiles of comparable-age outcomes across a defined cohort |
| Momentum / velocity | Change between recorded observations, not views divided by upload age |
| Binge / chain viewing | Actual sequential viewing requires private owner analytics; public structure is only a proxy |
| Automation / faceless | A production arrangement, not a growth or monetization guarantee |
| Content wall | A creator launch tactic; its causal value remains unvalidated here |
